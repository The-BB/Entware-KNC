    qualcommax: qca_ppe: report hardware MIB counters in port statistics
    
    The PPE forwards traffic between its ports in hardware, so frames that are
    bridged from one port to another never reach the host. The per-port
    software counters only ever account for the frames that were punted to the
    CPU, and the interface statistics consequently under-report bridged traffic
    by several orders of magnitude: transferring 1.10 GB between two ports of
    the same bridge on an IPQ8074 board leaves the receiving port reporting
    2724 transmitted bytes. Routed traffic crosses the host on its way between
    the two ports and is counted correctly, which is why this mostly goes
    unnoticed.
    
    Every frame does cross the port's MAC, which maintains its own MIB
    counters, and the driver already reads those for ethtool -S. Report them
    through get_stats64 as well, so that the interface counters reflect what
    actually crossed the wire, no matter which engine forwarded it. mt7530 and
    ar9331 report their port statistics from the hardware the same way, as do
    the ssdk and nss-dp vendor drivers for this hardware.
    
    A port is not bound to one MAC. Ports 5 and above drive either the GMAC the
    port number names or an XGMAC, and whichever block is idle reads back zero,
    so one MIB address per counter answers with zeroes for half the mux
    positions. Each row of the table therefore carries its counter twice, as
    the GMAC expresses it and as the XGMAC MMC block does, because the two
    disagree on address and on width: the GMAC keeps most counters in a single
    32-bit register and the XGMAC in a low/high pair. Where the XGMAC has no
    equivalent the row carries offset 0 and the counter reads zero, which is
    the honest answer for the collision counters, the alignment errors and the
    bad receive byte count - the XGMAC is full duplex and exposes only a
    good+bad octet total, and deriving a bad count by subtracting the good one
    would invent a counter the hardware does not keep. Two names shift meaning
    on such a port: the XGMAC lumps 1519-and-up into its 1024-to-max bin, so
    rx_1024_1518byte and tx_1024_1518byte hold the whole 1024-to-max range
    there and the 1519_maxbyte pair reads zero.
    
    Both MIBs are free running and the GMAC keeps most of its counters in 32
    bits, so the register value is not what rtnl_link_stats64 has to present: a
    port at line rate with minimum-sized frames wraps a packet counter in under
    49 minutes at 1G and in under five at 10G, and a total taken straight from
    the register steps backwards there. Each port therefore keeps a 64-bit
    total per counter alongside the raw value that total was last brought up to
    date from, and a fold adds the difference in at the width of the register
    it came from. get_stats64 and ethtool -S both fold before they answer, so
    neither lags the hardware and the two agree, and a delayed work folds every
    user port every 30 seconds so that a counter nothing reads cannot wrap
    twice unobserved.
    
    A difference is only meaningful across a counter that kept running, so two
    things take a baseline instead of a difference, and both take it at the
    moment the counter they read stops being the one the total was built from.
    qca_ppe_mac_config() asserts the port reset, which zeroes the MIB that port
    owns: the counters are banked before the reset, the rebase is held across
    it so that a fold arriving from another CPU cannot consume it while the
    counters are still running, and the baseline is taken from the cleared MIB
    as soon as the reset is released, while the MAC is still stopped. Deferring
    it to the periodic fold instead would discard everything the port counted
    from link-up. Left unguarded the reset lands every 32-bit counter on 2^32
    plus its true value and every 64-bit one back on zero, which is what the
    difference against a stale baseline comes to in each width.
    
    A port muxed to its XGMAC likewise starts reading a second MAC's counters,
    which advance independently of the first. qca_ppe_mac_link_up() banks what
    the MAC the port is leaving counted, changes the bank under mib_lock, and
    baselines the one it arrives on in the same critical section, so that the
    change costs neither the tail of the old MAC's counting nor the head of the
    new one's.
    
    The MIB block is reached through an MMIO regmap, whose accesses are
    spinlock protected and therefore safe in the atomic callers of
    ndo_get_stats64 - dev_get_stats() is reachable under a spinlock from
    bond_get_stats(). Every site takes mib_lock with softirqs disabled, so the
    lock has one acquisition mode rather than two. The two halves of a 64-bit
    counter are read in separate statements so that the order is not left to
    the compiler, low half first,
    which is the order the reference ssdk driver uses for them
    (adpt_hppe_get_mib_info(), adpt/hppe/adpt_hppe_mib.c,
    NHSS.QSDK.14.0.r8-00026-O). Nothing added here consumes the counters it
    reads: setup writes PPE_MIB_EN without PPE_MIB_RD_CLR, and MMC_CONTROL's
    reset value leaves RSTONRD clear.
    
    The XGMAC arm is inspection-only: there is no XGMAC port on the bench. The
    GMAC arm is what the board's copper ports exercise.
